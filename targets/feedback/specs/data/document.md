A website where the customers of a business that sells products give it their feedback.
It is for those customers, and for the staff who read what they say.
It lets the business know what to improve, catch unhappy customers, track satisfaction and collect testimonials.

## Problem

The business gets feedback from its customers by email and phone, in person, through surveys and forms, and through public reviews and social media.
Too little of it arrives: most customers never say anything.
What does arrive is scattered across those places, so nobody sees it all.
It arrives too late: the business hears of a problem after the customer has gone.
Nobody can see whether things are getting better or worse.
This is known from the business's own experience; nothing has been counted.

## Users

The customers: people who buy products from the business, and who give it their feedback. They give it today by email and phone, in person, through surveys and forms, and in public reviews and on social media.
The staff: the people in the business who read that feedback.

## Assumptions

That too little feedback arrives, that it is scattered and that it comes too late is the business's own experience, and nothing has been counted. The cheapest check is to count last month's feedback from each place it arrived.
That the online shop platform can tell the website who bought and when an order was delivered is not known yet. The cheapest check is to ask whoever runs the shop (Q10).

## Release plan

Release 1, within 3 months: customers give feedback on the website, staff read it, and customers of the online shop platform are asked when their order is delivered. The printed link and QR code cover everywhere else.
Later: requests for the business's own online shop and marketplaces.
