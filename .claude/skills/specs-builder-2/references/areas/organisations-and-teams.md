# Organisations and teams

- **What it is:** Whether data belongs to a person or to a group of people working together. It covers the workspace or organisation, inviting people into it, and what they share.
- **Applies when:** The product is sold to or used by groups: companies, teams, families, classes. It does not apply where each person's use is entirely their own.
- **If left unsaid:** Data is tied to individual users. When the first team wants to share, every record needs a new owner, and that migration is among the most expensive there is.
- **Ask:**
  1. When a customer uses this, is the customer a person or an organisation?
  2. If one person leaves the customer, should their work stay behind?
  3. Can one person belong to two organisations -- a consultant, an accountant, an agency?
  4. Who brings new people in, and how: an invitation, a link, a shared domain?
  5. Does the organisation have parts that must not see each other's work?
- **Options:**
  - personal accounts only -- simplest; right for a consumer product, and a dead end if teams will ever be the customer.
  - one organisation per person -- every user belongs to exactly one; covers most business products and keeps every screen simple.
  - a person in several organisations -- needed for consultants and agencies; every screen then needs to say which organisation it is showing.
  - organisations with teams inside -- for large customers; adds a second level of everything.
- **First release:** Decide who owns the data even if the first release has one person per organisation: if the customer is a company, the company owns it from day one. Invitations by email, one organisation per person. Teams inside an organisation wait.
- **Often forgotten:** An invitation to an address that already has an account, or to one that never accepts. Two organisations created by colleagues who did not know of each other. Who owns the organisation when its creator leaves. Whether a name, a logo or a web address identifies it.
- **Becomes:** Requirements for creating an organisation, inviting and removing people; a constraint on who owns data; the words workspace, organisation and member in the glossary; a non-goal for nested teams.
