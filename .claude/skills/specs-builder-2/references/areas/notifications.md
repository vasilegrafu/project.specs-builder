# Notifications

- **What it is:** What the product tells people when they are not looking at it. It covers email, text messages, push and in-product notices, and the user's control over them.
- **Applies when:** Something happens that a person needs to know about while away from the product: someone else acted, a deadline came, a job finished or failed. A product used only while the user is in it may need none.
- **If left unsaid:** Either nothing is sent and people miss what matters, or a message is sent for every event and people learn to ignore them all.
- **Ask:**
  1. What happens in the product that someone needs to know about within the hour? Within the day? Only the next time they look?
  2. For each: who needs to know, and what will they do when told?
  3. Where are they when it happens -- at a desk, on a phone, with no signal?
  4. What must the message say for them to act without opening the product?
  5. May they turn it off, and should any message be impossible to turn off?
- **Options:**
  - email -- reaches everyone and costs little; slow to be read, and competes with everything else in the inbox.
  - text message -- read within minutes; costs money per message, is tightly regulated, and has room for one sentence.
  - push on a phone -- immediate and free; needs an app installed and permission granted, which many refuse.
  - in the product only -- a badge or a list of what happened; right for what can wait until the next visit.
- **First release:** Email only, for the few events where someone must act and would not otherwise find out. Write each message's words in the document. Preferences per message type, digests and other channels wait until users say they are getting too much or too little.
- **Often forgotten:** A message that cannot be delivered. The same event told twice. A message arriving at night in the reader's time zone. Who the message appears to come from, and what happens when someone replies. Unsubscribing, which the law requires for anything not strictly about the service. A person told about something they no longer have access to.
- **Becomes:** A requirement per message with its trigger, reader and wording; a row of the Interface per message; a privacy quality requirement for what a message may contain; non-goals for the channels and controls left out.
