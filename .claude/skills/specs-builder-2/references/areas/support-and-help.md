# Support and help

- **What it is:** What a user does when they are stuck, and how you hear about it. It covers help content, a way to reach a person, and feedback.
- **Applies when:** Anyone outside the team uses the product. An internal tool whose users sit beside its makers needs little.
- **If left unsaid:** A stuck user has nowhere to go and quietly stops using the product, and the team never learns why. Or support arrives by every channel at once and nobody owns it.
- **Ask:**
  1. When a user is stuck, what do you want them to do?
  2. Who answers, and how soon can a user expect a reply?
  3. What will most people be stuck on? Can the product prevent it instead of explaining it?
  4. How will you find out about problems users do not report?
  5. Is support part of what customers pay for, with a promised response time?
- **Options:**
  - an email address -- one address, answered by the team; nothing to build, and the best source of learning there is at the start.
  - help written into the product -- a sentence where the confusion happens; prevents the question, and beats a manual nobody opens.
  - a help centre -- articles users search themselves; worth it once the same questions repeat.
  - chat or a ticket system -- faster for the user and trackable for the team; a commitment to staff it.
- **First release:** One visible way to reach a person, answered by someone who can also change the product, and a sentence of help wherever the first users got stuck. Keep a list of every question asked: it is the plan for the help centre and for the next release.
- **Often forgotten:** Where the contact link is when the product itself is broken or the user cannot sign in. Telling users about a known problem before they ask. What the user sees after sending a message. Questions that are really requests to do something on their behalf, which belong to the back office. A promised response time, which is a commitment with a cost.
- **Becomes:** A requirement for reaching support from anywhere, including signed out; an availability quality requirement if a response time is promised; wording in the Interface where users get stuck; non-goals for chat and a help centre.
