# Leaving

- **What it is:** How use of the product ends. It covers cancelling, closing an account, removing a person from an organisation, and what happens to the data afterwards.
- **Applies when:** People have accounts or pay. It is the area most often left out, because nobody designing a product imagines its users going.
- **If left unsaid:** There is no way to cancel except writing to support, which angers customers and in many places breaks the law. Data of people long gone stays forever.
- **Ask:**
  1. How does a customer stop paying? How does a person close their account?
  2. When they go, what happens to what they made: deleted, kept for a while, handed to someone else?
  3. If one person leaves an organisation, what happens to the things only they owned?
  4. Is there anything you must keep after they leave, for tax or legal reasons?
  5. May they come back later and find their data?
- **Options:**
  - by request -- they write and you do it by hand; acceptable for a handful of customers, and must still be honoured promptly.
  - cancel in the product -- a control that stops renewal; expected by users and required by consumer law in many places.
  - a grace period -- access ends, data is kept for a set time and can be reactivated; forgiving, and needs a real deletion at the end.
  - immediate deletion -- everything goes at once; simplest to explain, unforgiving of mistakes.
- **First release:** A way to cancel that does not need a conversation, a stated period after which data is deleted, and an export offered before it goes. Handing a leaver's work to a colleague can be done by hand at first.
- **Often forgotten:** Cancelling part way through a paid period. The last administrator of an organisation leaving. Data that must be kept by law after deletion is requested. Messages still being sent to someone who left. A returning user whose email address is "already in use". Asking why they left, once and briefly.
- **Becomes:** Requirements for cancelling, closing and removing a member with their criteria; a privacy quality requirement stating retention after leaving; a decision on the grace period; a non-goal for reactivation if it waits.
