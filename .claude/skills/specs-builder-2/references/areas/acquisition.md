# Acquisition

- **What it is:** How the people the product is for find out it exists and get from hearing of it to using it. It covers where they come from, what they see first, and the path to signing up.
- **Applies when:** The product must find its own users. It does not apply where users are told to use it by an employer, or the first customers are already signed by other means.
- **If left unsaid:** The product is finished and nobody arrives. Reaching users is treated as a later problem and turns out to be the main one.
- **Ask:**
  1. How will the first ten users hear of this? Can you name them?
  2. Where do people with this problem already look for help or talk to each other?
  3. What would you say to one of them in a sentence that makes them want to see it?
  4. Between hearing of it and using it, what must they do, and where could they give up?
  5. Who else has to agree before they can start -- a manager, a buyer, an IT department?
- **Options:**
  - by hand, one at a time -- you approach people you can name; slow and the only way to learn what persuades them; right for the first customers of almost anything.
  - a page that explains and collects interest -- one page with the promise and a way to leave an address; cheap, and a real test of whether the promise attracts anyone.
  - self-service sign-up -- anyone can start alone from the page; needed for many small customers, and makes onboarding carry all the weight.
  - through someone else -- a partner, a marketplace or an app store brings users; reach without marketing, on their terms and for their fee.
- **First release:** Name the first users and approach them directly. Put up a single page stating the promise, which also tests the wording. Open sign-up waits until people you did not approach are asking to get in.
- **Often forgotten:** The promise on the page matching what the product does on day one. The buyer and the user being different people who need different reasons. The sign-up form asking for more than is needed. What someone who is interested but not ready can do. Counting where users came from, starting with the first.
- **Becomes:** What is known about demand, in the Problem; assumptions about demand, each with its check; the journey from first hearing to first use; requirements for the page and sign-up if they are built.
