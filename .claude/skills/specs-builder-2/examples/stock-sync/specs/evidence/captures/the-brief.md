# The operations lead's brief, 2026-10-11

- **Given:** 2026-10-11, by Lea, who leads operations at Voltline, pasted into the conversation, with her answers to the questions asked the same day
- **Redacted:** nothing: the brief names no customer

The stock sync is the job that brings our stock in line with Nordkabel's feed every night. The contractor who wrote it has left. It moves to the new platform with the shop in the spring of 2027, and it must do there what it does today, less the holes below. Please write down what it does, so that whoever rebuilds it builds the same job.

What goes wrong today: in September 2026, 31 orders were cancelled because the product was not there to ship, about seven a week. Most were products Nordkabel had stopped listing, which the shop went on showing in stock. Three nights in September the run failed and nobody knew until a customer phoned; each of those mornings cost the person on call about three hours to find out what had happened and to run it again.

What should be better afterwards: orders cancelled for stock that was not there, under 2 a week by June 2027; no failed night that nobody knows of by 08:00, from the first month of the new job.

Who is involved: the operator on call, one of the three of us in operations, a week each in turn, who reads the morning mail and the runbook; the catalogue team, who set by hand the stock of what Nordkabel does not list; and Nordkabel, who rebuild the feed at 01:00.

Not now: a second supplier, ordering from Nordkabel when stock runs low, and prices from the feed. They are all on the list for later; the move is not the moment.

Keep it as it is: the run at 02:30; the refusals, which have saved us more than once; the exit codes, which the people on call know by heart; and the stock take, which comes once a year and is let through by hand as the runbook says.

What worries me: Nordkabel changed the feed's columns without a word in March, and nothing was synced for four nights.

We look back at a night's report or log for a month at most.

## Answers, 2026-10-11

Asked about what the job does that the runbook does not say:

- The low-stock mail should come every morning anything is low or out, whether or not anything changed that night. A quiet night that says nothing is how we missed the low ones.
- A run that stops for any reason must mail us. Give it an exit code of its own in the runbook; the ones we know stay as they are.
- The dry run is for reading before a stock take: it must show what would change even where the guard would refuse it, and it must never mail.
- The lines in the log of 10 October are the ones we read at 03:00; the new job writes them all.
- What Nordkabel stops listing: list those SKUs in the morning mail, and leave the number to the catalogue team. Nordkabel drops a SKU when it is discontinued, renamed or moved to another warehouse, and only the first means none left.
