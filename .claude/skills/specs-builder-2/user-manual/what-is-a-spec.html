Part 1: From zero

What is a specification?

If you've never heard the word, start here. It's a simpler idea than it sounds.

Think of building a house

Imagine telling a builder, "Make me a nice house." You'd get a house, but probably not the one in your head. So before anyone lays a brick, there are plans: how many rooms, where the doors go, what the kitchen needs. Plans don't build anything. They make sure that you, the architect, the plumber and the electrician all mean the same house.

Software is the same, with one difference: you can't see it being built, so misunderstandings stay hidden for longer. A specification, often shortened to spec, is the plans for a piece of software. It says in plain language what the product must do, and why.

What one looks like

At its simplest, a spec is a list of promises the product makes. Each promise is called a requirement. Here's one, for an online shop:

When a visitor selects Add to cart on a product in stock, the shop shall add it to their cart.

Notice three things about that sentence. It describes something you could see happen. It doesn't say how the programmers should do it. And shall is a tradition, meaning "this must be true".

A requirement also needs a way to tell whether it's been met. That's an acceptance criterion, and it follows a pattern as simple as a recipe card:

ExampleWhat it's doing
Giventhe product is in the cart with quantity 4,Sets the scene
When2 more are added,Says what happens
Thenthe shop refuses, and the cart is unchanged.Says what you should see

Anyone can check that by trying it. That's the point: a criterion ends arguments about whether something is finished.

What goes wrong without one

  • Everyone imagines something different, and finds out late, when it's expensive.
  • Decisions get made by accident, by whoever happens to be typing the code that day.
  • Nobody can say when it's done, because nobody wrote down what done means.
  • The reasons vanish. A year later, no one remembers why anything is the way it is.

Do you need to be technical?

No. A spec is about what the product does for people, so the person who knows the business is usually the best person to write it. In the past the hard part was the writing. With this skill, you talk and an AI does the writing. You decide.
