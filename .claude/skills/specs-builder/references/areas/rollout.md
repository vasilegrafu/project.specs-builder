# Rollout

- **What it is:** How a release reaches users. It covers who gets it first, how fast it widens, what happens to those using the previous version, and how to go back.
- **Applies when:** The work changes something people already use, or a new product is to be opened to more than a few people at once. A first product with no users has little to roll out, and everything to decide about who is first.
- **If left unsaid:** Everything is switched on for everyone on one day, and the first problem is found by all users at once with no way back.
- **Ask:**
  1. Who should have this first, and why them?
  2. What would you want to have seen before giving it to everyone?
  3. What changes for people who use the product today -- and have they been told?
  4. If it goes wrong on the first day, what do you do?
  5. Is there data or setup from before that must be carried across?
- **Options:**
  - everyone at once -- one date, all users; simplest, and fits a small change or a product with no users yet.
  - a named first group -- a few chosen customers use it and report before anyone else; the standard way to find what was missed.
  - gradual widening -- a growing share of users over days; limits the damage of a fault, and means two versions are live together.
  - opt-in beside the old -- users choose when to switch; kind to them, and the old way must be kept working until it is turned off.
- **First release:** A named first group who know they are first and whom you can speak to, then everyone. Say in advance what would make you stop. Gradual widening and opt-in wait for a user base large enough to need them.
- **Often forgotten:** Telling existing users before, not after. Existing data that does not fit the new rules. Support knowing what changed on the day. A way to turn the new thing off without a new release, which is a question for engineering. The end date of the old way. A date fixed by something outside: a contract, an event, a law.
- **Becomes:** The first release's outcome in the Release plan, and who receives it; a constraint for a fixed date; requirements for carrying existing data across; an open question for engineering on turning it off; a decision on what stops the rollout.
