# Onboarding

- **What it is:** What happens between a person's first arrival and the first time the product does something useful for them. It covers the empty first screen, setup, and the first success.
- **Applies when:** A new user has to do anything before the product is useful: enter data, connect something, invite someone, learn a concept. It matters less where someone else sets the product up for them.
- **If left unsaid:** The first screen is empty lists and a menu. The product works, and new users leave before finding out.
- **Ask:**
  1. What is the first moment a new user gets something they wanted? What must have happened before it?
  2. How long does that take today, and how long are they willing to give it?
  3. What do they need to have at hand -- data, a file, a colleague, a password to another system?
  4. Will they set it up themselves, or will you do it with them?
  5. What does the product look like with nothing in it?
- **Options:**
  - done for them -- you or your team set each customer up by hand; no building, and you learn what setup really involves; stops fitting when customers arrive faster than you can serve them.
  - guided setup -- a short sequence of steps that ends at the first useful result; the common choice, and each step you add loses some of the people.
  - sample data -- the product arrives filled with an example to explore; shows value before any effort, and must be easy to clear away.
  - nothing but good empty states -- each empty screen says what belongs there and offers the one action that fills it; cheapest, and enough when the first action is obvious.
- **First release:** Do it by hand for the first customers and write down every step you take: that list is the specification of the onboarding to build later. In the product, give each empty screen one sentence and one action.
- **Often forgotten:** The person who signs up and comes back a week later with nothing done. Setup that needs a second person who is not there. Importing what they already have. The email that confirms the account arriving late or in spam. Telling the difference, in your own numbers, between signed up and actually started.
- **Becomes:** The journey of the first use; requirements for each setup step and each empty state; a goal or metric for reaching the first useful result; a non-goal for the richer onboarding.
