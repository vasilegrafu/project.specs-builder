# Settings and profile

- **What it is:** What a user or a customer can change about themselves and about how the product behaves for them. It covers names, contact details, preferences and anything configurable.
- **Applies when:** A person's details can change, or users will reasonably differ in how they want the product to behave. It is small for most products and grows without limit if every disagreement becomes a setting.
- **If left unsaid:** A user cannot correct their own name or email address without writing to support. Or every open design question is turned into a setting, and nobody can say how the product behaves.
- **Ask:**
  1. What about a user can change over time: name, email, phone, language, time zone, employer?
  2. Which of those does the product depend on being right?
  3. Is there anything users will want to work differently from one another -- and is that a real difference, or a decision you have not made?
  4. Which settings belong to the person and which to their organisation?
  5. Who may change the ones that affect other people?
- **Options:**
  - nothing changeable -- details are fixed at sign-up and corrected by support; acceptable for a first few customers only.
  - a profile and no preferences -- users correct their own details; the product behaves one way for everyone; right for most first releases.
  - preferences for real differences -- time zone, language, units, notification choices; each one doubles a path to test.
  - settings for behaviour -- customers configure how the product works; powerful for varied customers, and each option is a decision passed to someone less able to make it.
- **First release:** A profile where users correct their own details, and settings only for facts about the user such as time zone and language. For every proposed behaviour setting, decide the behaviour instead and record the decision.
- **Often forgotten:** Confirming a changed email address before using it. A setting that changes what other people see. Sensible defaults, which are what nearly everyone will have. Where the setting is found again a month later. Changing a name that already appears on old records.
- **Becomes:** Requirements for changing each detail, with the confirmation and refusal cases; decisions in place of settings; a languages quality requirement; non-goals for configurability.
