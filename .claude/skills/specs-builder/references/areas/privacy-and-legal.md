# Privacy and legal

- **What it is:** What the law and your promises require of the product. It covers personal data, consent, terms of use, and the rights people have over what you hold about them.
- **Applies when:** The product holds anything about an identifiable person, takes payment, or serves a regulated field such as health, finance or children. For nearly every product with user accounts, it applies.
- **If left unsaid:** The product collects whatever is convenient and keeps it forever. The first request to delete someone's data, or the first customer's security questionnaire, cannot be answered.
- **Ask:**
  1. What does the product hold about people: names, contact details, locations, anything about health, money, beliefs, children?
  2. For each, why is it needed, and what would stop working without it?
  3. In which countries are the users, and which are your customers in?
  4. Has anyone qualified looked at this -- a lawyer, a data protection adviser, a customer's compliance team?
  5. What have you promised, or will you need to promise, in terms and a privacy notice?
- **Options:**
  - hold less -- do not collect what is not needed; the cheapest protection there is, and the only one that never fails.
  - standard terms and a privacy notice -- adapted from a reputable template and reviewed once; enough for most products selling to small customers.
  - built-in rights handling -- users export and delete their own data in the product; expected at scale, and usually done by hand at first.
  - formal compliance -- certification or an audit; demanded by large or regulated customers, and a project of months.
- **First release:** Write down every piece of personal data and why it is held, and drop what has no reason. Have terms and a privacy notice before the first outside user. Handle export and deletion requests by hand, with a written procedure. Treat anything in a regulated field as a question for a qualified person, never as a guess.
- **Often forgotten:** Data in backups, logs, emails and analytics tools as well as the main store. Other companies' services that receive the data. Consent for marketing being separate from consent for the service. Children. Keeping proof that someone agreed. This catalogue is general practice and not legal advice: what the law requires of this product is a question for someone qualified.
- **Becomes:** A privacy quality requirement stating what is held and for how long; constraints citing the law or contract; an open question for someone qualified in the law; requirements for consent, export and deletion; non-goals for certification.
