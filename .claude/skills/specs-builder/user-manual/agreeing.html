Part 4: Living with it

Agreeing it

Agreeing says: the person this is for has accepted it. The skill checks that nothing stands in the way, and then leaves the decision to you.

Is it ready?

python app/spec.py ready says whether it may be agreed, and lists every reason it may not:

  • additions not confirmed
  • open questions for the product side
  • qualities or risks left open, or a quality nobody has written
  • contradictions not settled, or findings that became nothing
  • flows whose story can't be told
  • questions from builders not taken in
  • anything stale
  • prototypes not looked at since what they show changed
  • anywhere the content falls short: a claim with nothing under it, a state nobody accounted for, a prototype that draws what the data doesn't hold

Open questions for engineering don't hold it up.

Agree it

Only the person it's for agrees it:

python app/spec.py do set document status agreed

This is refused while anything stands in the way. Once agreed, every change is refused until you set it back with do set document status draft. Agreeing also keeps the specification as it stands, so later python app/spec.py changes tells you everything that has moved since.

A good habit

Before you agree, read the list of additions and the non-goals. They hold what the skill assumed and what you ruled out.
