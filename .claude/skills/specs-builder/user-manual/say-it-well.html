How to say what you want

You don't need special wording. But a few habits make a big difference to what you get.

Let it ask

If you're not sure, say so. "Ask me about an invoice portal" is a perfectly good start. Claude asks the most deciding question first, and you can always answer "I don't know yet". Unknowns that get written down are harmless. The dangerous ones are those nobody noticed.

Give it what you already have

Do you have a website, a spreadsheet of rules, old documents or a price list? Point Claude at them. It reads them and notes where each fact came from, which saves you typing and gives you proof.

Read what it added

Claude fills in cases you didn't mention, and marks each as an addition. It's often right, and it's often the case you'd have forgotten. But it's your product. Read them, and say yes or no.

Tell it how it should look

Before Claude draws a single screen, it stops and asks about the look, every time. It never chooses one for you. It helps to have this ready:

  • Anything you already have: a logo, brand colours, a style guide, or a design system someone made for you.
  • What you like: websites, apps, screenshots or pictures you can point to. Say what you like about each. "I like how calm this one is" helps far more than "make it nice".
  • What you don't want: a competitor, or a style you dislike.
  • How it should feel: three words, and who it's for.
  • The theme: light, dark or both, and any colours you have in mind.

If you have none of this, say so. Claude will offer two or three directions with small samples, and recommend one. You choose. And if you'd rather not decide yet, it can draw plain grey screens first.

What a good requirement looks like

You won't usually write these yourself, but you should be able to judge them. Ask of each one:

  1. Could someone outside check it? "The cart shows the total" passes. "The code is clean" doesn't.
  2. Does it say one thing? An and in the middle usually hides a second requirement.
  3. Does it say what, not how? "A customer can pay by card" passes. "Use Stripe" is a choice for later.
  4. Is there a check for the bad case too? What if it's out of stock, empty or refused?
  5. Do you know why it's there? It should lead up to a goal. If it doesn't, ask whether you need it.
Trying it on your shop

R4 passes all five. You can check it, it says one thing, it doesn't mention technology, R4.2 and R4.3 cover the refusals, and it leads up to the goal of fewer abandoned checkouts.
