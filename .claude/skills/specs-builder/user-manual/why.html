Part 1: From zero

Why a document is not enough

So you know what a specification is. Here's why the usual way of keeping one goes wrong, and what this skill does differently.

The same fact in five places

Back to your shop. Usually a spec is a set of documents. A rule like up to five of a product ends up written in the product brief, the screen design, the description for programmers, the test plan and a page of business rules. Each was written by someone else, at a different time.

One day a customer who buys in bulk asks you for six, and you agree.

Nobody did anything wrong. But now the documents disagree, and they'll go on disagreeing:

  • A builder picks one. They read whichever document they have open, and build five or six.
  • A simple question has no quick answer. "What would changing this limit affect?" means someone rereading everything.
  • The reason gets lost. A year on, nobody knows whether five was a law, a stock rule or a guess.

The problem is the shape

More care doesn't fix this, because the problem isn't carelessness. A document is one long piece of text, so a fact inside it has no edges. Nothing can point at it, and nothing can tell when it moves.

Imagine instead that the rule lives in one place, as a thing of its own with a name, and everything that depends on it points at it. Change it once, and whatever points at it can be told.

Following the limit of five

In Voltline the limit is rule B1. Change it, and the skill names the 8 things that depend on it, straight away. Nothing has to be remembered. Part 4 makes this change for real.

Why this matters more now

AI assistants can now write much of the code. A person can hold a messy document in their head, and ask a colleague what was meant. An AI builds what it read, so a muddled spec gets built quickly and confidently.

Give it small, named facts and it can ask for exactly what one job needs, and ask back when something isn't settled.

What it doesn't do

It says what the product must do and why, never how to build it. It won't choose your technology or estimate your costs. And it doesn't decide for you: what it proposes is yours only once you confirm it.
